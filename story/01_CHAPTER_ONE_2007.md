## ▌ SCENE 1

```
EXT. OWENS VALLEY / LONE PINE, CALIFORNIA — "AFGHANISTAN" CAVE SET — DAY
MONDAY, MARCH 12, 2007 — 6:47 AM PT

Wind rips across the Alabama Hills. Red rock. Real dust. A real
cave mouth, dressed with real Stark Industries crates. No green
screen anywhere.

A 90-pound suit of hammered, bolted steel, the MARK I, stands
on a stand. It's practical, it weighs what it looks like it
weighs, and it is ugly on purpose.

JON FAVREAU (40), director, baseball cap, walkie on his hip,
circles it like it's a car he's thinking about buying.

ROBERT DOWNEY JR. (41) walks up in a torn, bloodstained tank
top, drinking a green smoothie. He knocks on the chest plate.
It CLANGS.

                    DOWNEY
          That's real.

                    FAVREAU
          That's real.

                    DOWNEY
          I'm going to be inside that.

                    FAVREAU
          You said no stunt double. I wrote
          it down. I had it notarized.

                    DOWNEY
               (grinning)
          I say a lot of things at 6 AM.

He runs his hand along a weld seam, then gets serious.

                    DOWNEY (CONT'D)
          Tony built this in a cave. With
          scraps. With a box of scraps.
          If it looks like a stunt guy is
          in there, the whole movie's a
          lie.

                    FAVREAU
          So you're in.

                    DOWNEY
          I've been out for ten years, Jon.
          I'm in.

A STUNT COORDINATOR hustles over with a harness rig.

                    STUNT COORDINATOR
          We rigged it for his weight plus
          ninety. Two guys on safety lines.
          If he goes down, he goes down
          slow.

                    DOWNEY
          Story of my career.
```

## ▌ SCENE 2

```
INT. MARVEL STUDIOS — 9242 BEVERLY BLVD — SECOND FLOOR — DAY
MONDAY, MARCH 12, 2007 — 10:15 AM PT

Downstairs, through the floor, a Mercedes salesman is closing
a deal. Up here: cramped offices, a coffee maker from 1994,
long boxes of comics stacked against every wall.

KEVIN FEIGE (33), Angels cap, bottle of Diet Coke, stands in
front of a WHITEBOARD taped over with butcher paper.

DAVID MAISEL (mid-40s, suit, no tie) closes the door and locks
it.

                    MAISEL
          Congratulations, Mr. President
          of Production.

                    FEIGE
          Variety spelled my name right.
          That's a first.

                    MAISEL
          Show me.

Feige tears the paper down.

Underneath: TEN CHARACTER NAMES, the Merrill Lynch collateral,
plus IRON MAN, HULK and THOR in red marker. Arrows everywhere.
All of them lead to one word circled three times in the middle:

                         AVENGERS

                    FEIGE
          Eight films. Four and a half
          years. Every one of them stands
          on its own. You could walk into
          any of them cold and you'd love
          it.
               (beat)
          And every one of them is a
          chapter.

                    MAISEL
          The bank thinks we're making one
          movie about a guy in a tin can.

                    FEIGE
          The bank is right. For now.

                    MAISEL
          Ike is going to ask what this
          costs.

                    FEIGE
          It costs nothing extra. That's
          the trick. We're making these
          movies anyway. We just make them
          in the same world. Same S.H.I.E.L.D.,
          same scientists, same timeline.
          Tony's Arc reactor exists in the
          Hulk movie. Nobody has to notice.
          Until they do.

Maisel steps closer. He sees something in the lower-right
corner of the board, a small sketch in purple marker:

A GAUNTLET. SIX EMPTY SLOTS.

                    MAISEL
          What's that?

                    FEIGE
          That's 2019.

                    MAISEL
               (laughs)
          Kevin. We might not exist in
          2009.

                    FEIGE
          Then it won't matter. But if we
          do...
               (taps the glove)
          Starlin. The Infinity Gauntlet.
          Thanos wipes out half the
          universe to impress Death because
          he's in love with her. We do it
          exactly like the comic. No
          watered-down version. No "he
          wants to save resources."
          He's a nihilist in love with a
          goddess.

                    MAISEL
          You're going to pitch Paramount a
          love story between a purple
          alien and the Grim Reaper.

                    FEIGE
          I'm not pitching anybody anything.
               (beat)
          That's rule one. Nobody outside
          this room says "universe." Not to
          agents, not to the trades, not to
          the actors. The contracts say
          "sequel options." The audience
          finds this on their own.

                    MAISEL
          And if they don't?

                    FEIGE
          They will. Fans always do.
          The first time Nick Fury walks
          into Tony Stark's living room,
          some kid on a message board is
          going to lose his mind. And then
          he's going to tell a million
          people.

                    MAISEL
          What's the project called?
          Internally.

Feige looks at the long boxes along the wall. Decades of Stan's
Soapbox columns.

                    FEIGE
          Bullpen.

                    MAISEL
          Project Bullpen.

                    FEIGE
          Stan would like it.

Maisel heads for the door, then stops.

                    MAISEL
          One more thing. Avi.

                    FEIGE
               (quiet)
          I know.

                    MAISEL
          He built this place.

                    FEIGE
          And he's going to leave it in
          good hands. I'll make sure of
          that.
```

## ▌ SCENE 3

```
INT. FAVREAU'S PRODUCTION TRAILER — LONE PINE — NIGHT
MONDAY, MARCH 12, 2007 — 8:40 PM PT

Favreau has storyboards spread across a folding table and is
eating cold craft-services pasta. His flip phone BUZZES.
CALLER ID: "KEVIN F."

                    FAVREAU
          Day one's in the can. Nobody died.
          Downey was in the suit for eleven
          takes.

                    FEIGE (V.O.)
               (over phone)
          Eleven?

                    FAVREAU
          He refused to come out. Said Tony
          wouldn't come out.

                    FEIGE (V.O.)
          That's why we hired him.
               (beat)
          Jon, I need an extra page.

                    FAVREAU
          We're locked. Paramount's got the
          script, the insurers have the
          script...

                    FEIGE (V.O.)
          It's not in the script. It goes
          after the credits.

Favreau puts his fork down.

                    FAVREAU
          After the credits. Nobody stays
          for that. The ushers are sweeping
          popcorn.

                    FEIGE (V.O.)
          They'll stay for this one.
          Samuel L. Jackson. Eye patch.
          Tony's living room.

                    FAVREAU
               (slow)
          Nick Fury.

                    FEIGE (V.O.)
          Bryan Hitch drew him as Sam
          Jackson in The Ultimates back in
          2002. Sam knows. He's been
          waiting five years for this call.

                    FAVREAU
          And he says what?

                    FEIGE (V.O.)
          "I'm here to talk to you about
          the Avengers Initiative."

A long silence. Favreau looks at the storyboard of the Mark I
he spent all day shooting.

                    FAVREAU
          Kevin. What are we making?

                    FEIGE (V.O.)
          A great Iron Man movie. That's
          all it has to be.
               (beat)
          Skeleton crew. Fake slate. We'll
          call it...
               (beat)
          "Rhodey's Birthday Party." Nobody
          looks twice at that.

                    FAVREAU
               (laughing)
          Terrence's birthday was
          yesterday.

                    FEIGE (V.O.)
          Then it's authentic.
```

## ▌ SCENE 4

```
INT. STAN LEE'S OFFICE — POW! ENTERTAINMENT — BEVERLY HILLS
WEDNESDAY, MARCH 21, 2007 — 2:00 PM PT

STAN LEE (84), in tinted glasses, is signing a stack of
posters with a black Sharpie. FEIGE sits across from him
holding a single envelope.

                    STAN
          Kevin! The man in the hat! What
          brings the President of
          Production to see an old
          huckster?

                    FEIGE
          Your cameo. Iron Man.

                    STAN
          Tell me I'm Hugh Hefner. I've
          been practicing.

                    FEIGE
               (smiles)
          Close.
               (slides the envelope over)
          And one more thing. Just for you.
          Read it and give it back.

Stan opens it. One page. He reads it. His Sharpie stops moving.

                    STAN
               (softly)
          All of them.

                    FEIGE
          All of them.

                    STAN
          Same world. Like the comics. Like
          1963. The Fantastic Four in the
          Baxter Building and Spidey
          swinging past the window and the
          Hulk on the news...

                    FEIGE
          We don't have the Fantastic Four.
          Or Spider-Man. Yet.

                    STAN
               (grinning)
          "Yet." I like this guy.
               (hands the page back)
          You know what we called it in the
          '60s? The Marvel Universe. The
          fans named it. We didn't. The
          kids wrote letters: "Hey Stan, is
          this the same New York where
          Daredevil lives?" And we said,
          "True Believer... it always was."

                    FEIGE
          That's the plan. They name it.
          Not us.

                    STAN
          Excelsior, kid.
```

## ▌ SCENE 5 (CLOSING)

```
INT. MARVEL STUDIOS — FEIGE'S OFFICE — NIGHT
THURSDAY, MAY 31, 2007 — 11:52 PM PT

The building is empty. The Mercedes showroom below is dark.

FEIGE (33) is alone. AVI ARAD's farewell card is propped on the
desk: "Don't screw up my toys. — Avi."

Feige unlocks a bottom drawer and takes out a three-ring binder.
Hand-lettered on the spine:

                      THE CODEX

He opens it. The first tab: IRON MAN — MAY 2, 2008.
Last tab, still blank except for a date:

                      MAY 2019

He picks up a purple marker and writes one line on the blank
page:

   "He courts Death. She never answers. That's the whole saga."

He closes the binder. Locks the drawer. Turns off the light.

In the dark, the whiteboard still shows the purple gauntlet
sketch. Six empty slots.

                                              CUT TO BLACK.

                    SUPER: "1 YEAR, 11 MONTHS UNTIL
                    IRON MAN OPENS."

                    (beat)

                    SUPER: "12 YEARS UNTIL HE SNAPS."
```
