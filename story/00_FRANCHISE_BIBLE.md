## 1. THE STUDIO: MARVEL STUDIOS

| | |
|---|---|
| **Founded** | **1996**, by **Avi Arad**, when Marvel Films (est. 1993) was reorganized as Marvel Studios |
| **Reborn as an independent studio** | **Tuesday, September 6, 2005**: a **$525 million** non-recourse credit facility with **Merrill Lynch**, put together by **David Maisel** |
| **Headquarters (2007)** | 9242 Beverly Blvd, Beverly Hills, on the floor above a Mercedes-Benz dealership |

**Why 1996 is the right year:** Marvel spent the bankruptcy years (1996–1998) being offered money for its characters by every studio in town. Arad set up Marvel Studios so the company would **never sell a single film right.** Studios wanted to rent Spider-Man, the X-Men, the Fantastic Four and Blade, and Marvel turned all of them down. The 2005 Merrill Lynch deal was the payoff: **Marvel finances and owns its own movies.** The loan was secured by the film rights to ten characters and properties, which are only a small part of a library of more than 5,000 that Marvel owns outright: **Captain America, The Avengers, Nick Fury, Black Panther, Ant-Man, Cloak & Dagger, Doctor Strange, Hawkeye, Power Pack and Shang-Chi.**

---

## 1A. THE RIGHTS: MARVEL OWNS EVERYTHING (LOCKED)
- **Marvel owns 100% of its characters.** No film rights have ever been sold or licensed. That includes Spider-Man, the X-Men and every mutant, the Fantastic Four, Doctor Doom, Galactus, the Silver Surfer, Namor, Blade, Daredevil, the Punisher, Ghost Rider and everyone else.
- **There are no earlier Marvel theatrical films.** No *Blade* (1998), *X-Men* (2000), *Spider-Man* (2002), *Daredevil* (2003), *Hulk* (2003) or *Fantastic Four* (2005). Every character appears on screen for the first time in this universe.
- **This is the first shared cinematic universe in film history.** No studio has tried anything like it. The industry has no word for it yet, and the fans will have to invent one.
- **What this means for Thanos:** Galactus, the Silver Surfer, Mephisto, Adam Warlock, Doctor Doom, the mutants and the Fantastic Four are all available at full comic power for the Infinity Saga. **Nobody is left out, and nobody is nerfed.**

## 1B. CONTINUITY: EARTH-616 (LOCKED)
The whole universe is adapted from **Earth-616**, the main Marvel Comics continuity. Earth-1610 (Ultimate), movie-only versions and "reimagined" origins are not allowed. Where the 616 comics have updated a detail over time (Marvel's sliding timescale), we use the latest 616 version.

**Earth-616 anchors:**
| Character | 616 canon we follow |
|---|---|
| **Nick Fury** | Jack Kirby's Fury: WWII sergeant of the **Howling Commandos**, lost his eye to a grenade and later became Director of **S.H.I.E.L.D.** (Supreme Headquarters, International Espionage, Law-enforcement Division). The **Infinity Formula** keeps him in his fifties. **Cast: Samuel L. Jackson (b. Dec 21, 1948), the one casting exception in the whole saga.** His history, powers and personality are 100% 616. |
| **Iron Man** | Tony Stark, captured by insurgents and builds the Mark I with **Ho Yinsen**. The war is Afghanistan (the sliding-timescale update in *Extremis*, 2005). **Obadiah Stane is Iron Monger**, and the **Mandarin** and his ten rings of alien origin are part of Tony's world. |
| **The Hulk** | Bruce Banner is caught in the blast of the **gamma bomb** while saving **Rick Jones**. The madder Hulk gets, the stronger Hulk gets, **with no upper limit.** Hulk is part of the Avengers from day one. |
| **Thor** | The Odinson, sent to Earth by Odin to learn humility, first living as the mortal **Dr. Donald Blake**. **Mjolnir** is enchanted with "Whosoever holds this hammer, if he be worthy..." |
| **Captain America** | Steve Rogers, Project: Rebirth, **Dr. Abraham Erskine**, with **Bucky Barnes** at his side. He is lost at the end of the war and **frozen in ice until the Avengers find him.** |
| **The Avengers** | The founding line-up from *Avengers* #1 (1963): **Iron Man, Thor, the Hulk, Ant-Man (Hank Pym) and the Wasp (Janet van Dyne).** They form to fight **Loki.** Cap is thawed out later and joins. |
| **Mutants** | Born with the X-gene. **Charles Xavier and Magneto (Max Eisenhardt)** are there from the start. |
| **Thanos** | Jim Starlin's 616 Thanos (see Section 7). |

## 3. DISTRIBUTOR & ACQUIRER

### Distributor: **PARAMOUNT PICTURES**
- 2005 deal: Paramount distributes and markets for a flat fee (about 8%). **Marvel keeps ownership, final cut and the sequel rights.**
- Paramount is hungry, reorganizing under Brad Grey, and doesn't ask for creative control. It's the ideal partner for a studio with a secret.
- **Covers:** Phase 1 through 2011.
- **Covers every Phase 1 film, *The Incredible Hulk* included.** Marvel has never licensed a character to another studio, so no outside studio holds a claim on any film.

### Acquirer: **THE WALT DISNEY COMPANY**
- **Monday, August 31, 2009**: Disney announces it is acquiring Marvel Entertainment for **about $4.0 billion** in cash and stock. CEO **Bob Iger** (b. Feb 10, 1951, age 58) closes the deal on December 31, 2009.
- Disney buys Paramount's distribution rights to **The Avengers (2012)**. **Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures** distributes everything from Phase 2 on.
- **Deal term (canon):** Marvel Studios keeps full creative autonomy. Feige keeps final say on story and continuity. Disney handles marketing, parks, merchandise and international reach.

**Why Disney:** it has the global reach to make a 29-film saga an event without absorbing it into the Disney brand. It's the only buyer with both the money and the patience for a ten-year plan.

## 4. STRUCTURE: SERIALIZED vs. STANDALONE

### The target: **60% SERIALIZED / 40% STANDALONE**

It ramps up by phase, so audiences are gradually trained to expect connection:

| Phase | Years | Films | Serialized | Standalone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1** | 2008–2012 | 8 | 35% | 65% |
| **Phase 2** | 2013–2015 | 9 | 60% | 40% |
| **Phase 3** | 2016–2019 | 12 | 80% | 20% |
| **Saga total** | 2008–2019 | **29** | **≈61%** | **≈39%** |

### THE FIVE LAWS OF THE CODEX (continuity)
1. **Every film is somebody's first film.** It has to work completely on its own, and the connective tissue is a bonus.
2. **Real-time rule.** Each film takes place in the year it's released unless it's explicitly set in the past. Characters age on their real birthdays.
3. **Scars stay.** Injuries, losses, trauma and tech upgrades carry forward and are logged.
4. **Death is permanent** unless the comics give an explicit, established mechanism for a return.
5. **Nobody gets nerfed.** Every hero and villain shows up at comic-accurate power levels. When a character loses, it's because of story, strategy or a mismatch. It's never because the character was written down to make someone else look good.

## 5. GENRE, RATING, RUNTIME

**Core genre:** superhero action-adventure.
**House rule:** every film **wears a second genre**, so no two installments play the same.

| Hero | Subgenre |
|---|---|
| Iron Man | Techno-thriller / military-industrial drama |
| The Hulk | Fugitive thriller / monster movie |
| Thor | Shakespearean mythological epic |
| Captain America | WWII war epic, then a 1970s paranoid-conspiracy thriller |
| The Avengers | Ensemble war film / disaster epic |
| Black Panther | Royal succession drama / espionage |
| Doctor Strange | Occult horror-fantasy |
| Ant-Man | Heist caper |
| Guardians / Cosmic | Space opera |

**Rating:** **Hard PG-13** is the default. Stakes are real, violence has consequences, and there's blood when the story calls for it. Street-level characters (Blade, Punisher, Daredevil, Moon Knight) are held for a future **R-rated** label so they never have to be watered down.

**Runtime (long but reasonable):**
- Solo origins: **140–160 min**
- Sequels / team-ups: **155–175 min**
- Phase-ending crossovers: **180–200 min**
- **Hard ceiling: 3 hr 20 min**

## 6. PRODUCTION DOCTRINE
- **Actors do their own stunts and motion capture.** Stunt teams train with them, rig them and keep them safe. They don't replace them.
- **100% practical first.** Real suits, real sets, real explosions, real locations. CGI and VFX are used only where physics or safety require it, such as cosmic realms, the Hulk and Thanos, and they're built on top of practical references and on-set mo-cap.
- **Shoot on location first.** Soundstages are only for what can't exist in the real world.

## 7. THE BIG BAD: **THANOS, THE MAD TITAN**

**First appearance:** *Iron Man* #55 (Feb. 1973), created by **Jim Starlin** (with Mike Friedrich).

### Comic-accurate canon (LOCKED)
- **Origin:** an **Eternal of Titan**, son of **A'Lars (Mentor)** and **Sui-San**, brother of **Eros (Starfox)**. He was born with the **Deviant Syndrome**, which gives him his gray, ridged look.
- **Motivation:** he is **in love with Mistress Death.** He kills half of all life as a **courtship offering** to win her. **It is not about resources or overpopulation.** Death is a character in this saga, and she never speaks to him.
- **Power level (no nerfs):** before he ever touches a Gem, Thanos can trade blows with the Hulk and Thor, knows cosmic science beyond Tony Stark, and is a master strategist. With the **Infinity Gauntlet** he is **omnipotent**. The heroes lose because the Gauntlet is literally godhood, not because they're weak.
- **The six Infinity Gems** (called **"Gems"** on screen, never "Stones"): **Soul, Mind, Power, Space, Time, Reality.** The Soul Gem is tied to **Adam Warlock.**
  - **Comic colors (locked):** Soul **green** · Mind **blue** · Power **red** · Space **purple** · Time **orange** · Reality **yellow**.
  - **Origin:** first called **"Soul Gems"** (the Soul Gem debuted in *Marvel Premiere* #1, 1972). They were named the **Infinity Gems** in *Silver Surfer Annual* #2 and *The Thanos Quest* (1990). They are the remains of a single being that existed before the universe and chose to shatter itself.
  - **The Gauntlet:** Thanos's **Infinity Gauntlet** is a plain gold glove with no special properties of its own. All the power comes from the Gems.
- **His people:** **Gamora** (adopted daughter, "the deadliest woman in the galaxy"), **Nebula** (his self-proclaimed granddaughter), and **the Black Order.**
- **The fatal flaw** (Starlin's thesis, spoken by Adam Warlock): *"Deep down, Thanos believes he doesn't deserve to win. He always leaves himself a way to lose."*
- **Endgame players, all comic-accurate:** Adam Warlock, the Silver Surfer, Mephisto, Nebula, and cosmic entities at full power.

### THE SAGA NAME: **THE INFINITY SAGA**
Taken directly from Starlin's run, *The Thanos Quest* → *The Infinity Gauntlet* → *The Infinity War*. It isn't the name of a franchise, so it can be said out loud. It's the name of a story, and the public won't learn it until the right moment.

**Seeding plan:**
- **Phase 1:** a Gem hides in plain sight. **Final shot of Phase 1:** a purple hand, a golden glove, and one line: *"To challenge them... is to court Death."*
- **Phase 2:** *The Thanos Quest.* He starts collecting.
- **Phase 3:** *The Infinity Gauntlet*, a two-part finale.

## 8. THE SECRET
- **No one outside Project Bullpen hears the word "universe."** Actor contracts carry **multi-picture options** described as "sequel protection."
- Post-credit scenes are shot with **skeleton crews** under **fake slate names**, and the pages are handed out on set and collected afterward.
- **The franchise has no name.** The press and fans will name it. Feige's standing order: *"When they name it, we'll know we've won."*
