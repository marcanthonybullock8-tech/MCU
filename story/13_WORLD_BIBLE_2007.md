# THE WORLD BIBLE: AMERICA & HOLLYWOOD, UP TO DAY ONE
### The real-world story's world, as it stands on **Monday, March 12, 2007**
### STATUS: 🔒 LOCKED as world canon. ✅ **The real-world story has started** (Chapter One opens April 28, 2006, in this world).

> **How to read this document.** Our world is **the real world**, with its real history, real people, real headlines and real prices. **Three pebbles** have been dropped into it. Everything unique about this world is **a ripple from one of those three pebbles.** If something isn't a ripple, it happened exactly the way it happened in real life.

---

## 0. THE THREE PEBBLES

| # | The pebble | Where it lands |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Marvel never sold a single film right.** Avi Arad founded Marvel Studios in 1996 so the company would own its movies, and **no Marvel character has ever appeared in a theatrical film.** | Hollywood's whole idea of what a comic-book movie is |
| **2** | **Donohue Entertainment exists.** Founded in 1966 by Victor Donohue in a converted Crenshaw laundromat, it's **the largest Black-owned entertainment company in America.** | Los Angeles, the music business, television, and every room the Bullock kids walk into |
| **3** | **The Ensemble exists.** Ten fictional actors, plus the Donohue–Bullock family, and every show, song and film they've ever made. | American pop culture from 1986 to 2007 |

**Everything else is real.** George W. Bush is president. The Iraq War is in its fifth year. Hurricane Katrina happened. YouTube, MySpace and the iPod happened. The only question this bible answers is **what the pebbles changed.**

## 1. THE COUNTRY: THE UNITED STATES ON DAY ONE

### The mood: "Something is about to break"
America in March 2007 feels like **a room right before the power goes out.** Nothing has collapsed yet, but everyone can hear the building creak.

| Area | Where things stand on Monday, March 12, 2007 (real history) |
|---|---|
| **Politics** | **President George W. Bush**, second term, approval in the low 30s. On **January 4**, Democrats took both houses of Congress and **Nancy Pelosi** became the **first woman Speaker of the House.** On **January 10**, Bush announced **the "surge"** of more than 20,000 more troops to Iraq. |
| **The 2008 race** | **Hillary Clinton** announced on **January 20.** A first-term Illinois senator, **Barack Obama**, announced on the steps of the Old State Capitol in Springfield on **Saturday, February 10, 2007**. |
| **The economy** | The Dow is around **12,300.** On **February 27** it fell **416 points** in one day. Housing prices have stopped rising. **This week**, subprime lender **New Century Financial** is collapsing (the NYSE halts its stock on **March 13**; bankruptcy follows April 2). Almost nobody outside Wall Street knows the words "subprime mortgage" yet. |
| **Technology** | On **January 9**, Steve Jobs announced **the iPhone** (on sale June 29). **MySpace** is the biggest social network in the country. **Facebook** opened to everyone on September 26, 2006. **Google bought YouTube** for $1.65 billion in October 2006. **Twitter** is having its breakout moment at **SXSW in Austin, this very week** (March 9–13). |
| **Television** | ***American Idol*** season 6 is at its peak (Sanjaya is still on). ***Heroes*** (NBC) is the hottest new show. ***The Sopranos*** begins its final season on April 8. *Grey's Anatomy*, *Lost*, *24*. |
| **The aftermath** | **Hurricane Katrina** hit on **August 29, 2005.** Nineteen months later, **New Orleans is still half-empty.** |
| **Prices** | Gas **≈ $2.55/gal** · the average movie ticket is **$6.88** (2007) · the federal minimum wage is **$5.15** (it goes up to $5.85 on **July 24, 2007**) · a first-class stamp is **39¢** |

### Where the pebbles touch the country
| Real event | The ripple |
|---|---|
| **Katrina, August 29, 2005** | It hit on **Elxa Bullock's 25th birthday.** She canceled her party and turned that night's ***Elxa After Dark*** into **a live six-hour telethon** on D1 (see Section 4). It raised **$31 million.** **Joan Donohue**, born in New Orleans' Seventh Ward, has since spent **$40 million** of family money rebuilding **212 homes** there through the **Mercer Homes** project, named for her father. She goes back every month and never lets it be filmed. |

| **The iPhone, January 9, 2007** | **Marc-Anthony Bullock** bought Apple stock in **1998** at about **$13 a share**, when the analysts had written Apple off. After two stock splits, **each share he bought is now worth about $350**, roughly **27 times** what he paid. He hasn't sold one. |
| **The Google IPO, August 2004** | Marc bought in at **$85.** On Day One it trades around **$450.** |

## 2. THE BIG RIPPLE: A WORLD WITHOUT MARVEL MOVIES

### The missing films
**In our world, none of these films were ever made.** In real history they grossed about **$2 billion in North America**, and more than twice that worldwide.

| Missing film | Real release | What happened here instead |
|---|---|---|
| *Howard the Duck* | 1986 | Nothing. **Marvel has never had a movie, not even a bad one.** |
| *Blade* | 1998 | **Nobody proved an R-rated Black superhero could open.** |
| *X-Men* | 2000 | **Hugh Jackman** is **a Tony-winning Broadway star** (*The Boy from Oz*, 2004) and "the guy from *Kate & Leopold*." Nobody has ever called him Wolverine. |
| *Spider-Man* / *Spider-Man 2* | 2002 / 2004 | **Sony never had its biggest franchise.** Its 2002 slot went to **nothing that anyone remembers.** **Sam Raimi** is still, to Hollywood, "the *Evil Dead* guy." |
| *Daredevil*, *X2*, *Hulk* | 2003 | The only live-action Hulk ever made is still **Bill Bixby and Lou Ferrigno** on CBS (1977–82). That's the comparison **Amond Baker** is about to face. |
| *The Punisher*, *Blade: Trinity* | 2004 | – |
| *Elektra*, *Fantastic Four* | 2005 | – |
| *X-Men: The Last Stand* | 2006 | – |
| *Ghost Rider* | February 16, 2007 | Presidents' Day weekend 2007 went to other films. |
| ***Spider-Man 3*** | **May 4, 2007** | ***The biggest hole of all*** (see Section 6). |
| *Fantastic Four: Rise of the Silver Surfer* | June 15, 2007 | – |

**What Marvel's characters *have* been in this world:**
- **Comics**, since 1939 (Timely), 1961 (Marvel).
- **Saturday-morning cartoons.** An entire generation grew up on ***X-Men: The Animated Series*** (1992–97) and ***Spider-Man*** (1994–98) on Fox Kids, produced by Marvel's own animation arm. **In this world, that's the only place kids have ever seen Marvel heroes move.**
- **Old TV**: *The Incredible Hulk* (1977–82), a 1977 *Spider-Man* series, two 1979 *Captain America* TV movies, and the 1998 TV movie ***Nick Fury: Agent of S.H.I.E.L.D.*** with David Hasselhoff, which fans bring up when they want to feel pain.
- **Toys and video games.** ToyBiz merged with Marvel in 1998 and kept the lights on.
- **Stan Lee** is **the most famous comic-book creator alive**, but he has **never had a movie cameo**, because there has never been a movie. In 2006 he hosted ***Who Wants to Be a Superhero?*** on the Sci Fi Channel (real). To most Americans he's **"the Excelsior guy."**

### Hollywood's conventional wisdom: "THE CAPE CURSE"
Without *Blade*, *X-Men* and *Spider-Man* to prove otherwise, **every studio in town believes superhero movies are box-office poison.** The evidence, as any executive will list it for you:

| The case against capes | Year | The lesson Hollywood took |
|---|---|---|
| ***Batman & Robin*** | 1997 | *"Nipples on the Batsuit. The genre is a joke."* |
| ***Steel*** (Shaquille O'Neal) | 1997 | *"$1.7 million. Black superheroes don't sell."* |
| ***Catwoman*** (Halle Berry) | 2004 | *"Proof. Again."* |
| ***Superman Returns*** | 2006 | *"A $200 million-plus budget and about $200 million domestic. Even Superman can't fly anymore."* |

**The evidence the industry explains away:**
| Hit | Year | The excuse |
|---|---|---|
| ***Batman Begins*** ($205M domestic) | 2005 | *"That's a Christopher Nolan crime movie. It worked because it pretended not to be a comic-book film."* |
| ***The Meteor Man***, ***Blankman*** | 1993, 1994 | *"Comedies. Don't count."* (They also didn't make money.) |
| ***Spawn*** ($55M domestic, a Black lead) | 1997 | Almost nobody in the industry remembers it did fine. |
| ***Sin City***, ***V for Vendetta***, ***Hellboy*** | 2004–06 | *"Graphic novels. Auteur projects. Different thing."* |
| ***300*** (**$70.9M opening, a March record**) | **Friday, March 9, 2007** | **Three days before Day One.** Warner Bros. is calling it *"a war movie."* Nobody in the trades uses the word "comic." **Kevin Feige saw it twice that weekend**, and didn't say a word to anyone. |

**So on Day One, Marvel Studios isn't just making its first movie. It's betting $140 million against the one thing all of Hollywood agrees on.**

### The fans
- **Comic fandom is a subculture that believes Hollywood hates it.** Its homes are **SuperHeroHype**, **Ain't It Cool News**, **Comic Book Resources** and **Newsarama.**
- **San Diego Comic-Con** (July 26–29, 2007, around 125,000 people) is still mostly about **comics, TV and sci-fi.** **Hall H has never seen a Marvel movie panel.**
- **The fans have waited their whole lives** for a real Marvel movie. They're **thrilled, terrified and deeply suspicious** of anything that changes the comics. **Every casting announcement is a war** on the forums.
- The recurring forum voices we already know: **XxWolverineXx** (a purist skeptic whose screen name is the name of a hero **who has never been in a movie**), **ExcelsiorFaithful**, **PepperPottsFanClub**.

### The seventh sister: DONOHUE ENTERTAINMENT
The trades have a nickname for it: **"the seventh sister."** It's **not a major**, but **every major has tried to buy it**, and **it has turned every one of them down.** In the whole industry **only two companies have famously refused to sell what they own: Donohue Entertainment and Marvel.** (Victor Donohue and Avi Arad have met exactly once. They got along *very* well.)

| Division | Founded | What it is in 2007 |
|---|---|---|
| **Donohue Records** | **1966**, in a converted laundromat at Crenshaw & 43rd | The original. A soul, R&B and hip-hop catalog of **more than 9,000 masters, all owned outright.** |
| **Donohue Pictures** | **1972** | **About six films a year**: family dramas, comedies, prestige pictures. **Robert Bullock**'s *Harbor Street* (1994) was its first Oscar nominee for Best Director. |
| **The Crenshaw Lot** | **1972** | **31 acres** in South Los Angeles: **nine soundstages**, a backlot "Main Street," and the original laundromat, **kept as a museum.** During **the 1992 riots**, Victor kept the gates open, **paid every employee**, and fed the neighborhood from the commissary for six days. **Nobody touched the lot.** |
| **Donohue Television** | **1979** | Sitcoms and dramas for the networks, including ***The Mercers*** (ABC, 1986–93). |
| **Donohue Talent** | **1983** | A boutique talent management company. **Natasha Bullock manages her own children through it.** It's feared across town. |
| **D1** | **1994** | **A Black-owned cable network** with 72 million homes. **BET was sold to Viacom in 2001; D1 has never been sold.** Its flagship is ***Elxa After Dark.*** Slogan: ***"Ours."*** |
| **The Donohue Foundation** | **1988** | Scholarships, **Mercer Homes** (New Orleans), and the **Crenshaw Film Academy**, a free film school for South L.A. teenagers. |

**House rule, painted over the commissary door:** ***"Own the masters. Own the land. Own the name."***
**Net worth:** about **$4.1 billion** (Victor, 2007).
**The social event of Hollywood's year:** **Thanksgiving at the Donohue estate** in View Park. **Invitations are handwritten by Joan.** Getting one means you've made it. **Losing one** is industry news.

### Black Hollywood in 2007 (real, with one addition)
- **Two weeks before Day One**, at the 79th Academy Awards (**February 25, 2007**), **Forest Whitaker** won Best Actor (*The Last King of Scotland*) and **Jennifer Hudson** won Best Supporting Actress (*Dreamgirls*). **Eddie Murphy** was nominated too. The industry calls it **"a big night"** and then goes back to making the same decisions.
- **Will Smith** is **the biggest movie star in the world** (*The Pursuit of Happyness*, 2006). **Denzel Washington** is an institution. **Tyler Perry** is building his own studio in Atlanta.
- **The executive suites are still almost entirely white.** The **"urban" budget ceiling** is real. **The overseas-box-office excuse** kills Black-led projects every week.
- **The addition:** **Donohue Entertainment**, where **all of that is different**, and everyone in town knows it.

### Power and money
- **2006 domestic box office: about $9.2 billion.** DVD sales are at their **all-time peak**. The home-video money pays for Hollywood.
- **The "threequel summer" of 2007** is coming: *Shrek the Third* (May 18), *Pirates of the Caribbean: At World's End* (May 25), *Ocean's Thirteen*, *Rush Hour 3*, *The Bourne Ultimatum*. **One threequel is missing**: *Spider-Man 3.*
- **The storm on the horizon:** the Writers Guild's contract **expires October 31, 2007.** Everyone in town knows **a strike is coming.** It starts **November 5, 2007.**

### Marvel's long winter (1996–2005)
- **December 1996:** Marvel files for bankruptcy (real).
- **1996:** **Avi Arad** founds **Marvel Studios** with one rule: **never sell a film right.** Every studio in town calls with offers **for Spider-Man, the X-Men, the Fantastic Four and Blade.** **Every offer is turned down.** In the trades, Arad is called ***"the man who said no to $100 million."***
- **1998:** **ToyBiz** merges with Marvel (real). The toys and the cartoons **keep the company alive.**
- **2000:** **Kevin Feige** joins Marvel Studios, a company with **no movies** and no plans to make any. **He reads everything, keeps notes on all of it, and starts building a plan on paper.**
- **September 6, 2005:** **Merrill Lynch.** **Marvel will finance its own films.**
- **September 29, 2006:** **Marc-Anthony Bullock** is cast as Tony Stark. **SuperHeroHype's servers crash.**
- **Friday, March 9, 2007:** ***300*** opens to $70.9M. **Feige** sees it twice.
- **Monday, March 12, 2007:** **Day One.**
