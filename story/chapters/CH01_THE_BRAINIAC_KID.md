## ▌ COLD OPEN

```
INT. MARC-ANTHONY BULLOCK'S HOUSE — KITCHEN — BALDWIN HILLS,
LOS ANGELES — PRE-DAWN
FRIDAY, SEPTEMBER 29, 2006 — 5:51 AM PT

A big, warm kitchen that nobody decorated. Cereal boxes lined
up like trophies. A framed Sundance laurel propped against the
toaster. Through the window, the lights of the city are still on.

MARC-ANTHONY BULLOCK (24, looks 19) is at the stove in a
Crenshaw High hoodie and pajama pants covered in cartoon
rocket ships. He has not slept. He is making pancakes for the
DOCUMENTARY CREW (a camera operator, a boom op, and the
DIRECTOR, who is always off-camera).

He flips a pancake. It lands perfectly.

                    MARC-ANTHONY
          Seven for seven. You guys want
          chocolate chips? I'm doing a
          chocolate chip one. I'm doing a
          chocolate chip one whether you
          want it or not, because I'm
          about to lose my entire
          [BLEEP]ing mind.

His BLACKBERRY buzzes on the counter. He jumps. Looks at it.

                    MARC-ANTHONY (CONT'D)
          It's my mom. It's just my mom.
               (answers)
          Hi Mommy. — No. — No, I'm up. —
          Nine minutes. I know. I can see
          the clock, I have a clock, I'm
          looking at it right —
               (listens)
          ...I love you too.
               (hangs up; to camera)
          She called to tell me it's in
          nine minutes. Like I didn't know
          it's in nine minutes. I've known
          it's in nine minutes for FOUR
          HOURS.

He slides the pancake onto a plate and hands it to the boom
operator, who has to take it one-handed.

                    DIRECTOR (O.S.)
          What happens in nine minutes?

Marc looks at the lens for a long second. Then he grins, the
grin of a kid who's about to get in trouble.

                    MARC-ANTHONY
          In nine minutes, a press release
          goes out on the East Coast. And
          then everybody finds out. And
          half this town is gonna laugh.

                         (beat)

                    MARC-ANTHONY (CONT'D)
          Let 'em.

He turns off the burner.

                         HARD CUT TO BLACK.

                         TITLE CARD:
                    "FIVE MONTHS EARLIER"
```

## ▌ SCENE 1

```
INT. PINNACLE ARTISTS AGENCY — BARRY KESSLER'S OFFICE —
CENTURY CITY — DAY
FRIDAY, APRIL 28, 2006 — 11:32 AM PT

Corner office, 31st floor. Glass. Framed one-sheets on every
wall: BIG MOUTH (1997). PLEDGE WEEK (2004). The Brainiac
cast photo, signed "TO UNCLE BARRY."

BARRY KESSLER (57), deep tan, reading glasses on a chain,
the kind of agent who has sent the same fruit basket for
thirty years. He loves Marc-Anthony like a son. He also has
not had a clue what to do with him for fourteen months.

Marc-Anthony sits across from him, with his feet on the chair
next to his. The documentary CAMERA sits in the corner. This is
the crew's fifth day with him.

Barry slides a SCRIPT across the desk. The cover page reads:

     "DOUBLE BOGEY"

                    BARRY
          Before you say anything. Before
          you say one word. Warner Bros.
          Two point five against five. And
          Owen Wilson wants to do it.

Marc picks up the script and flips to the last page, then to
the first page, then puts it down.

                    MARC-ANTHONY
          Barry. I'm a caddy.

                    BARRY
          You're the caddy! You're the
          heart of it!

                    MARC-ANTHONY
          On page four, a golf cart runs
          over my [BLEEP]. On page
          sixty-one, a goose runs over my
          [BLEEP]. I'm counting. That's
          a franchise. That's the
          [BLEEP]-getting-run-over
          trilogy.

                    BARRY
          Comedy is pain.

                    MARC-ANTHONY
          Comedy is pain, yes. It's not
          PAIN specifically to the —

                    BARRY
          — it's the eighth comedy you've
          said no to, Marc.

A beat. Marc takes his feet off the chair.

                    MARC-ANTHONY
          Eighth. You're counting too.

                    BARRY
               (gentler)
          Kid. I've had you since you were
          five. You were in the cereal ad.
          The one with the spoon. I love
          you. I'm telling you as somebody
          who loves you. The town has two
          pictures of you in its head. The
          Brainiac kid...

                    MARC-ANTHONY
          ...and the Brainiac kid who says
          [BLEEP] on HBO.

                    BARRY
          The drama didn't work. I know the
          drama was beautiful. My wife cried.
          My wife doesn't cry at anything,
          she didn't cry at our wedding.
          But four point two million, Marc.
          Four point two.

                    MARC-ANTHONY
               (quiet)
          I know what it made.

Barry's computer PINGS. He glances at it. It's the Variety
headline. He almost doesn't mention it, then does.

                    BARRY
          Marvel's making a movie. You see
          this? Iron Man. Favreau's
          directing.

Marc goes very still.

                    BARRY (CONT'D)
          Crazy people. Putting their own
          money on a comic book. You know
          what Superman cost? And Superman,
          people have HEARD of.

                    MARC-ANTHONY
          Tony Stark is an engineer.

                    BARRY
          Who's Tony Stark?

                    MARC-ANTHONY
          Iron Man. He's an engineer. He's
          a genius engineer who drinks too
          much and talks too much and he
          builds himself a suit in a cave
          with a box of scraps because he's
          dying.
               (beat)
          I have a mechanical engineering
          degree, Barry.

                    BARRY
          I know. I was at the graduation.
          I cried.

                    MARC-ANTHONY
          You said your wife doesn't cry.

                    BARRY
          I CRY. My wife doesn't cry.
               (beat; he sees Marc's face)
          No. No, no, no. Marc. It's a
          comic book. Comic book movies are
          poison right now. Everybody in
          this building will tell you. If
          it bombs, it follows you forever.
          You'd be "the Brainiac kid who
          was in the comic book bomb."

                    MARC-ANTHONY
          At least it'd be a new sentence.

He stands. Picks up DOUBLE BOGEY. Puts it back on Barry's
desk, gently, like he's tucking it into bed.

                    MARC-ANTHONY (CONT'D)
          Tell Owen I said he's gonna be
          great.
```

```
                    ELXA
```
