## CASTING RULES (LOCKED)
1. **The ten fictional-actor roles:** Tony Stark, Bruce Banner, Steve Rogers, Thor, Hank Pym, Janet van Dyne, Claire Barton, Natasha Romanoff, Wanda Maximoff and Pepper Potts are played by the fictional ensemble in this bible.
2. **Every other Marvel character is played by the real-life actor who played them.** Samuel L. Jackson is Nick Fury (the one exception to the 616 rule on casting).
3. **When more than one real actor played a role, the most iconic or longest-serving one gets it for the whole saga, and nobody is recast mid-saga.** Examples: **Don Cheadle** as James "Rhodey" Rhodes, **William Hurt** as Thunderbolt Ross, **Jon Favreau** as Happy Hogan, **Jeff Bridges** as Obadiah Stane.
4. **Characters whose real-life portrayals came from films that don't exist in our reality** (Fox's X-Men and Fantastic Four, Sony's Spider-Man, and similar): **real actors play them, and the showrunner picks which actor when there was more than one.** They come in as each character enters the story.
5. **Showrunner picks (locked):** **Josh Brolin** is **Thanos**. **Aubrey Plaza** is **Mistress Death** (the only real actor to have played Marvel's Death), silent from *Iron Man* (2008) on. The **X-Men** are Patrick Stewart (Xavier), Ian McKellen (Magneto), Tye Sheridan (Cyclops), Sophie Turner (Jean Grey), Nicholas Hoult (Beast), Shawn Ashmore (Iceman), Ben Hardy (Angel), Evan Peters (Quicksilver), Ray Park (Toad) and Peter Dinklage (Bolivar Trask). The **Fantastic Four** are Pedro Pascal (Reed), Vanessa Kirby (Sue), Joseph Quinn (Johnny) and Ebon Moss-Bachrach (Ben), with **Robert Downey Jr. as Doctor Doom**, Kerry Washington (Alicia), Paul Walter Hauser (the Mole Man) and Jeffrey Wright (the Watcher). **Spider-Man** is Tom Holland, with Rosemary Harris (Aunt May), Martin Sheen (Uncle Ben), Tony Revolori (Flash Thompson) and Elizabeth Banks (Betty Brant). **Mephisto** is Ciarán Hinds. **Tilda Swinton** is the Ancient One. **Laurence Fishburne** plays both **Goliath** (Bill Foster) and **the voice of the Silver Surfer**. **Zendaya** is **Mary Jane Watson**. **Annette Bening** is the Kree **Supreme Intelligence**. **The All-New X-Men** are Alexandra Shipp (Storm), Daniel Cudmore (Colossus), Kodi Smit-McPhee (Nightcrawler), Caleb Landry Jones (Banshee) and Blair Redford (Thunderbird).
6. **In memory:** **Chadwick Boseman died on August 28, 2020.** Following real history, **T'Challa is never recast.** He died in the MCU (off-screen, of illness, in 2020), and **Letitia Wright's Shuri is Black Panther** from Phase 4 on.
7. **Casting exceptions (the only two in the saga):** **Samuel L. Jackson** as Nick Fury, and **Chris Evans** as **Mar-Vell**, cast so that Mar-Vell is male as on 616.
8. Even with real actors, **every character is still 100% Earth-616 and nobody is nerfed.** The actor is the only thing that comes from real life.

## THE DONOHUE–BULLOCK DYNASTY

### VICTOR DONOHUE, the Patriarch
| | |
|---|---|
| **Born** | May 30, 1936, Chicago, IL (South Side) |
| **Age** | **70** · *Looks: mid-50s* |
| **Titles** | Founder & Chairman, **Donohue Entertainment** (1966–present); CEO (1966–1996) |
| **Married** | Joan Mercer, 1960 |
| **Net worth (2007)** | ≈ **$4.1 billion** |

A crooner out of the Chicago supper-club circuit, Victor had three gold records and a string of B-movie leading roles by 1962. Hollywood never gave him an A-picture, so in **1966, at age 30, he built his own company.** Donohue Entertainment started as a record label in a converted Crenshaw laundromat. It grew into film and TV production, a talent agency, and eventually one of the most important Black-owned entertainment companies in America. In this world, Donohue Entertainment is **what Motown would have become if it had also bought a movie studio.** Victor is warm, very shrewd, and he never forgets a slight. He still comes in to the office every Monday. He has one rule, and his grandchildren can recite it: *"Own the masters. Own the land. Own the name."*

### JOAN DONOHUE (née Mercer), the Matriarch
| | |
|---|---|
| **Born** | April 17, 1938, New Orleans, LA |
| **Age** | **68** (69 on Apr 17) · *Looks: early 50s* |
| **Married** | Victor Donohue, 1960 |

A New Orleans jazz singer turned screen actress, known for the 1960s melodramas that made audiences cry into their popcorn. In **1974 she became one of the first Black women to direct a feature for a major studio**, and she won an Emmy directing television in the 1980s. She's the family's conscience and its toughest critic. Her grandchildren are more afraid of Joan's silence than of Victor's temper.

### NATASHA BULLOCK (née Donohue), the Mother & Manager
| | |
|---|---|
| **Born** | June 2, 1961, Los Angeles, CA *(same birthday as Kevin Feige, which comes up later)* |
| **Age** | **45** · *Looks: early 30s. People regularly mistake her for Elxa's older sister.* |
| **Titles** | CEO, Donohue Entertainment (1996–present); personal manager for all three of her children |
| **Married** | Robert Bullock, 1981 |

The only child of Victor and Joan. She was an 1980s R&B/pop star and film actress, with a Grammy, two Golden Globe nominations and a signature role as a jazz singer in *Blue Hour* (1987). She took over as CEO at **35** and turned Donohue into a modern media company. As a manager she is **feared in every agency in town**. She has walked her children out of three signed deals because the contracts had morality clauses written to target Black talent. She loves hard, negotiates harder, and has never lost her temper in public.

### ROBERT BULLOCK, the Father & Director
| | |
|---|---|

| **Age** | **48** · *Looks: late 30s* |

A UCLA film school graduate and a quiet perfectionist. He's an **Oscar-nominated director** (Best Director nomination, *Harbor Street*, 1994) of rich, humane family dramas. He shoots on real locations, uses long takes, and rehearses more than anyone else in town. He's the one who taught Marc-Anthony that the camera catches whatever you're thinking, even when you don't say it. He's calm, funny once you know him, and still hopelessly in love with his wife.

## THE BULLOCK CHILDREN

### 🔴 MARC-ANTHONY BULLOCK
**MARVEL ROLE (LOCKED): Tony Stark / Iron Man**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Born** | January 5, 1982, Los Angeles, CA (Baldwin Hills) |
| **Age** | **25** · *Looks: 19–20* |
| **Birth order** | Second child, oldest son of Natasha & Robert Bullock; grandson of Victor & Joan Donohue |
| **Occupations** | Actor · singer · screenwriter · filmmaker · producer |
| **Fame (2007)** | ★★★★ **A-List**, but for the wrong things |
| **Reported IQ** | **250** |
| **Future net worth** | **Over $990 billion, and it goes up every single day** *(future canon: locked)* |

**Who he is:**
Marc-Anthony is the **sweetest, kindest, most loving, compassionate, innocent and pure-hearted man** in Hollywood. Crew members who have worked with him describe him the same way: *"He learns your kids' names."* He's also **the funniest person in any room.** His comedy is unhinged, unfiltered and gleefully offensive, and it spares nobody, including himself. The contrast is the key to him. His mouth has no brakes, and his heart has no bad bone in it.

He has natural presence on camera and in person. He's known for his natural appearance, distinctive features and effortless charisma, and paparazzi say he has never taken a bad photo.

**The dream:**
His number-one goal, ahead of Oscars, money or anything else, is **a family. A big, gigantic family.** Kids are non-negotiable. He believes in **generational wealth** the same way his grandfather does: build it, own it, pass it down. On every set he's on, the kids end up following him around, and it's been that way since he was one of the kids.

**Career timeline:**
| Year | Age | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| 1987 | 5 | First national commercial (a cereal ad). Meets Amond Baker at the audition. |
| 1991–1995 | 9–13 | ***SPLAT!*** (Nickelodeon), a kids' sketch-comedy show. Breakout cast member with Amond and Tayia. |
| 1994 | 12 | Tests at a **reported IQ of 250** during a *60 Minutes* segment on child prodigies, and the number follows him everywhere after that |
| 1995–1999 | 13–17 | ***Brainiac*** (Disney Channel), starring as Marcus Hayes, a 13-year-old genius who skips straight to college. The top show on the network. Two Kids' Choice Awards and a Daytime Emmy nomination. |
| 1996 | 14 | Finishes high school through on-set tutoring |
| 1997 | 15 | ***Big Mouth*** (family comedy, $118M domestic) |
| 1998 | 16 | Puts his *Brainiac* earnings into Apple stock, buying at about $13 a share while analysts were writing Apple off |
| 2000 | 18 | Debut album ***Late Bloomer***: platinum, and a Grammy nomination for Best New Artist |
| 2000–2003 | 18–21 | ***The Marc Bullock Show*** (Nickelodeon), a teen variety show. He's head writer at 18. |
| 2003 | 21 | **B.S. in Mechanical Engineering, USC**, *summa cum laude*, earned while filming full-time |
| 2004 | 22 | ***Pledge Week***, an R-rated college comedy and a $96M hit. Critics: *"Watching the Brainiac kid say that word is like watching your nephew smoke."* |
| 2004 | 22 | Buys into the **Google IPO** (August 2004) |
| 2005 | 23 | Writes, directs and stars in ***The Long Way Home***, an indie drama about a father and son in Crenshaw. **Sundance Special Jury Prize**, and **Independent Spirit Award, Best First Screenplay.** It made $4.2M at the box office, which is a flop by studio standards. |
| 2006 | 24 | HBO stand-up special ***Unsupervised***. Huge ratings, and a parents'-group boycott. He gives all his backend to Children's Hospital Los Angeles. |
| **Sept 29, 2006** | 24 | **Cast as Tony Stark in *Iron Man*** (Marvel Studios / Paramount). The industry reaction: *"Marvel bet $140 million on the Brainiac kid."* |
| **Mar 12, 2007** | 25 | *Iron Man* begins principal photography. He does his own stunts in the 90-lb practical Mark I. |

**The problem Iron Man ended (typecasting and a dry spell)**
Before Marvel called, he went **14 months** without a greenlit project (July 2005 – September 2006). To the studios he's either *"the Brainiac kid"* or *"the Disney kid who curses now,"* so every script his agency sends is a comedy. He has turned down **eleven** of them. *The Long Way Home* proved he can do drama, but almost nobody saw it. He was too famous to be treated as a newcomer and too typecast to be taken seriously. **Iron Man is his shot at proving he can grow up on screen, and half of Hollywood is waiting for him to fail.**

**🔒 SIGNATURE TRAITS (LOCKED CANON):**
1. **He becomes synonymous with Tony Stark.** By the end of the Infinity Saga, the public can't separate them. Fans, the press and eventually the culture treat "Marc-Anthony" and "Tony Stark" as the same person. Kids dress as Tony and say they're dressing as Marc. When a real-world headline needs a genius billionaire, it reaches for his face. It starts on the day he's cast and never stops.
2. **The improviser.** He ad-libs constantly, on every set, in every take. Some of Tony Stark's most famous lines are **his, not the script's.** Directors learn to shoot a scripted take and then **"one for Marc,"** and the "Marc take" is often the one that makes the movie. Writers learn to leave gaps for him, the way the *Brainiac* writers once did. Continuity supervisors keep a separate log of his ad-libs. It drives some co-stars crazy and makes others better, and it's all over the press tours and blooper reels.
3. **The snack smuggler.** He **hides snacks all over every set**: in props, behind flats, inside workbench drawers, and eventually in his own suits. Crews find stashes months later. A Tony Stark scene where he's eating becomes a running in-joke, because very often the snack was his own and the eating wasn't in the script. Snack hunts become a set tradition, and prop masters start leaving him "legal" hiding spots.

**Relationships:**
- **Amond Baker:** best friend since the cereal commercial in 1987. Brother. Right-hand man.
- **Grace Bullock:** his little sister and his ride-or-die.
- **Esther Smilley:** a close friend since childhood. 🔒 *Future canon: they get together in 2011 and marry in 2013. Not yet.*
- **Tayia Boyd:** co-star on *SPLAT!* and *Brainiac*. They have a great working relationship and read each other's timing without talking about it.

| **Occupations** | Actress · television personality |

**Backstory:** Elxa was the child star of ***The Mercers*** (1986–1993), the family sitcom her grandmother Joan directed for Donohue Television, and she won a Young Artist Award at 9. She's so successful as a host that casting directors don't see her as an actress anymore. She hasn't had a film role since 2003. *"She's a personality"* is the most polite version of what gets said in casting meetings. She's the glamorous, funny, fearless one in the family, and she's quietly hungry to prove she's still an actor.

### 🟢 AMOND BAKER
**MARVEL ROLE (LOCKED): Bruce Banner / The Incredible Hulk**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Born** | February 10, 1982, Inglewood, CA |
| **Age** | **25** · *Looks: 19* |
| **Occupations** | Actor · singer |
| **Fame (2007)** | ★★★ **Known Face** (nostalgia fame) |

**Backstory:** He met Marc-Anthony at the 1987 cereal-commercial audition when they were both 5, and they've been **inseparable since.** He was on *SPLAT!* (1991–1995) and played Marcus's roommate on *Brainiac*. His solo R&B album ***Quiet Storm*** (2003) was well reviewed but only sold about 180,000 copies. Tired of being the funny sidekick, he enrolled in **Juilliard's Drama Division at 20** and graduated in **2006**. He's a serious, disciplined, **deeply gifted dramatic actor.**

**Personality:** gentle, soft-spoken, anxious and thoughtful. He keeps his feelings under tight control, and when something finally breaks through, it's **frightening.** Directors who have seen it don't forget it. He's Marc's steady opposite: Marc is the explosion and Amond is the pressure underneath.

**2007 problem: typecasting and a dry spell**
His UPN sitcom ***Baker's Dozen*** was cancelled in 2006 when UPN merged into The CW. Hollywood still sees him as *"the funny best friend from SPLAT!"* Nobody has read his Juilliard reviews, and he has been out of work for nine months. **That ends on April 12, 2007, when Marvel announces him as Bruce Banner in *The Incredible Hulk*.** Filming starts in Toronto that summer.

🔒 *Future canon: together with Harmony Divine in 2013, married 2015; daughter Lyric born April 20, 2014.*

### 🔵 TYRESE AVERY
**MARVEL ROLE (LOCKED): Steve Rogers / Captain America**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Born** | July 24, 1981, Fort Bragg, NC |
| **Age** | **25** · *Looks: 20* |
| **Occupations** | Pop superstar · actor |
| **Fame (2007)** | ★★★★★ **Global Icon** |

**Backstory:** He's the son of **Sgt. First Class Harold Avery** (U.S. Army, 82nd Airborne) and a church choir director, and he grew up on bases from North Carolina to Germany. He was signed at 16 after a church-choir video circulated through the industry, and his debut single ***"Stand Tall"*** (1998) went to No. 1 in 23 countries. Since then: four albums, **more than 45 million records sold, five Grammys**, and sold-out stadium tours on four continents. He is clean-cut, sincere, polite to a fault, and a real believer in doing the right thing. His fan base, "the Averys," is the biggest in pop.

**2007 problem: nobody takes his acting seriously**
He has made two films. ***Starlight*** (2003) was a musical romance that critics tore apart (*"a two-hour music video"*). ***Fast Lane*** (2005) was an action movie that flopped with a $38M domestic gross. The industry verdict is that he's *"a pop star in a costume."* He's the most famous person in this cast and the one with the least credibility as an actor. Privately, it's what hurts him most.

### ⚪ ESTHER SMILLEY (later Bullock)
**MARVEL ROLE (LOCKED): Thor Odinson** *(female; she has always been female)*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Born** | September 20, 1982, Atlanta, GA |
| **Age** | **24** · *Looks: 16–17* |
| **Occupations** | Actress · singer |
| **Fame (2007)** | ★★★★ **A-List** as a *memory* |

**Backstory:** She's **"the modern-day Shirley Temple."** She was discovered at 3 singing at an Easter pageant in southwest Atlanta, and at 5 she starred in ***Sunny*** (1988), a tap-dancing musical that made $140M and made her America's little sweetheart. After that came ***Sunny Goes to Washington*** (1990), ***Sugar Hill Sweetheart*** (1992), and a **Golden Globe nomination at age 9**, the youngest in the category's history. She met Marc-Anthony when they co-starred in the Disney holiday film ***The Kid Who Stole Christmas*** (1992), and they've been close friends ever since. She's known for natural beauty, distinctive features and effortless elegance. She's warm and gracious, has a dry wit, and **is far more competitive than anyone realizes.**

**2007 problem: stuck as the child star**
Every adult role she tries fails in the same way. ***Magnolia Falls*** (2003) was a Southern drama in which she played a widow; audiences laughed at her in the trailer. ***Loose Change*** (2005) was a romantic comedy, and critics wrote *"we can't see past the dimples."* Her youthful looks make it worse: she's 24 and gets carded for R-rated movies. She's made a quiet living voicing animated films. **Nobody in Hollywood can picture her as a warrior god, and that is exactly why the role would work.**

🔒 *Future canon: together with Marc-Anthony in 2011, married 2013; twins Emma & Victoria born May 10, 2012.*

Since *Floor Work*, **every script she's offered is a dance movie.** She wants dramatic, dangerous parts with real depth, and Hollywood only wants her to dance. She has turned down four dance movies this year.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Born** | February 3, 1982, Chicago, IL (Chatham) |

| **Occupations** | Actress |

**Why she's Pepper:** Tony and Pepper's back-and-forth is the most important relationship in Iron Man's corner of the universe, and Tayia and Marc-Anthony have been doing that rhythm on screen since they were nine. With them it's instinct, not acting.
